# Aggregation Failure

A classic problem in philosophy of social science: summing individually
optimal choices does not reliably yield an optimal collective outcome. The
usual examples are resource-allocation (commons problems, Condorcet
paradoxes); [[Theory Sandpile]] is a clean case of the same structure in an
**epistemic** domain instead.

[[Drive Rule - Avoid Crowded]] is individually rational — avoiding a theory
where credit is already claimed under the [[Theory-Scientist Mapping|priority
rule]] — and collectively costly, in the specific, quantified sense worked
out in [[Novelty-Seeking Phase Transition]].

> [!tip] The value of the quantified version
> You can ask exactly *how much* worse — mean avalanche size roughly 2.5×
> larger, activity dropping by nearly half — rather than just asserting a
> qualitative mismatch between individual and collective rationality.

Methodological-individualism debates ask whether social facts reduce
without remainder to facts about individuals and their choices. This model
doesn't settle that debate, but it supplies a case where the reduction is
total (every macro-number is computed from agent-level rules with nothing
added) and the aggregation is still visibly surprising — which is itself a
data point about how much methodological individualism can explain on its
own.

See [[Social Epistemology of Credit]] for the credit-specific version of
this argument, and [[Emergence as a Tractable Case]] for the companion
question about what kind of macro-pattern this even is.

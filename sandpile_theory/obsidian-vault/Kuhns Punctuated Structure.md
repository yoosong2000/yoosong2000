# Kuhns Punctuated Structure

Kuhn's picture in *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* — long normal
science, punctuated by revolutions — has always had an explanatory gap: why
punctuation at all, and why does revolution-size vary the way it seems to
historically?

[[Self-Organized Criticality]] answers a narrower but sharper question:
punctuation and graduated change aren't two different kinds of process
needing two different explanations (an incrementalist story for normal
science, a special story for revolutions). They're **the same distribution
read at different scales** — see the `P(s) ~ s^-τ` scaling form in
[[Self-Organized Criticality]] and its fit in
[[Truncated Power-Law Fitting]].

> [!important] The actual philosophical move
> This dissolves an apparent dichotomy (gradualist vs. revolutionary change)
> into one mechanism with one exponent, rather than resolving it by
> argument.

> [!warning] The honest limit
> This explains the *shape* of the size distribution, not why any
> particular revolution happened — which is the part historians of science
> actually argue about. A power law tells you revolutions-of-every-size are
> expected; it says nothing about which theory, which year, which people.

Compare [[Emergence as a Tractable Case]] for the more general point about
what this kind of structural explanation can and can't do.

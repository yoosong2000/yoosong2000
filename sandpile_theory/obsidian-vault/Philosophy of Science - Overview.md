# Philosophy of Science - Overview

What kind of philosophical research question [[Theory Sandpile]] is actually
powerful for, and where it has nothing to say.

The model is strongest exactly where a philosophical question can be reduced
to a claim about **aggregation** — what collective pattern follows from a
stated micro-rule — and weakest wherever the question needs semantic content
(truth, meaning, justification) the model has no representation for.

## Four clusters where it does real work
- [[Social Epistemology of Credit]] — does individual rationality under a
  winner-take-all reward system produce good collective search?
- [[Aggregation Failure]] — the general philosophy-of-social-science problem
  this is an instance of
- [[Kuhns Punctuated Structure]] — a mechanism, not just a metaphor, for
  normal science vs. revolution
- [[Emergence as a Tractable Case]] — a formally checkable instance of
  "weak emergence" to reason from

## Where it has nothing to say
Anything needing a truth predicate. Realism/anti-realism, verisimilitude,
whether science converges on true theories rather than merely crowded ones
— the model has no concept of a theory being *right*, only developed.
Individual belief-updating and confirmation theory are out of scope too:
there's no evidence, no credence, nothing resembling Bayesian updating, just
attention allocation. Incommensurability in Kuhn and Feyerabend's strong
sense (conceptual, not just positional, distance between paradigms) is at
most gestured at by the bridging-theory requirement in
[[Theory-Scientist Mapping]] — that captures *structural* distance in a
graph, not meaning change.

> It's a formal object for testing whether a proposed reward structure for a
> research community is epistemically self-defeating — a live, contested
> question in social epistemology rather than a settled one.

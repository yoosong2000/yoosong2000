# Research Questions

[[Theory Sandpile]] is a mechanism model — a fixed 2D lattice, a conserved
population, no domain content in what a "theory" actually contains. It's
built to answer *comparative and structural* questions, not to forecast a
real field.

## What it's good for

- **Mechanism sensitivity** — which features of the credit rule actually set
  τ? Is the [[Novelty-Seeking Phase Transition|transition at novelty_weight ≈ 0.5]]
  meaningful, or an artifact of this particular scoring function?
- **A falsifiable empirical claim** — real research bursts (citation
  cascades, a sudden crowd into a subfield) should show a
  [[Truncated Power-Law Fitting|truncated-power-law]] size distribution
  under novelty-seeking, and a bump-shaped, non-scale-free one under pure
  [[Drive Rule - Avoid Crowded|crowd-avoidance]]. Checkable against
  bibliometric data.
- **Does the transition survive a realistic topology?** — theory space
  isn't a 2D grid. Rerun on a small-world or scale-free graph (`GraphSpace`
  in place of `GridSpace`) and see whether it washes out.
- **Heterogeneous populations** — what happens with a mixture of strategies
  in the same field at once, instead of one archetype at a time?
- **Is critical actually good?** — reframe from "is it critical" to "is
  there a `novelty_weight` that maximizes distinct theories developed per
  scientist-step" — closer to what the welfare side of
  [[Social Epistemology of Credit|this literature]] actually argues about.

## What it's not good for

Predicting a specific field's trajectory, dating a specific discovery, or
treating the credit numbers as real citation counts. The model has
structure, not domain content — see
[[Philosophy of Science - Overview#Where it has nothing to say]].

# Social Epistemology of Credit

The model's home literature: Kitcher (1990), Strevens (2003), Zollman
(2010), Weisberg & Muldoon (2009) — full citations in [[References]].

The question: given that credit is scarce and winner-take-all (see
[[Theory-Scientist Mapping]]), what allocation rule for scientists-to-topics
serves the community's epistemic good, and does individual rationality get
you there automatically?

## The counter-example this model produces

[[Drive Rule - Avoid Crowded]] is the individually rational response to
winner-take-all credit — and it's the rule that breaks scale-free search,
producing rarer, more catastrophic cascades instead (numbers in
[[Novelty-Seeking Phase Transition]]).

That's a formal instance of the "invisible hand" optimism in Polanyi's
*Republic of Science* and Kitcher's own early argument failing on its own
terms: rational self-interest under this reward structure does *not*
converge on good collective search. It needs an additional ingredient
([[Drive Rule - Novelty-Seeking|novelty-seeking]]) to do that.

> [!tip] Why run it instead of arguing it in prose
> A philosophy paper arguing against naive invisible-hand pictures of
> science usually has nothing but intuition pumps for this claim. The model
> gives it a mechanism *and* a number: τ from 0.91 to 0.52, mean avalanche
> size up 2.5×, right at one parameter threshold.

See also [[Aggregation Failure]] for the more general pattern this is a
special case of.
